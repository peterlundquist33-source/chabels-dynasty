SEASON PREVIEW

Every roster, read through this league's own scoring — not a generic ranking.

The Format, In One Breath

Chabels isn't a standard points league. Blocks and steals are worth 4 each — double most formats — so rim-protecting, ball-hawking bigs and guards are worth more here than their real-NBA reputation suggests. Made/attempted shots are split (FGM +2 / FGA −1, FTM +1 / FTA −1), so volume without efficiency actively costs you, while high-efficiency, high-usage players print. Double-doubles pay 5, triple-doubles pay 10, and offensive boards get a bonus on top of the standard rebound point — so stat-stuffers who fill multiple categories a night are worth more than one-dimensional scorers. That's the lens every preview below is written through.

PG Jamal Murray #9 · SG Jaylen Brown #13 · SF Jaylen Brown #10 · PF Giannis Antetokounmpo #1 · C Alperen Şengün #3

Giannis might be the single most dominant format fit in the whole league — a two-point shooting percentage freak who also rebounds, blocks, and racks up double-doubles on autopilot, and he's only 31, so the clock isn't closing on him yet. Şengün gives a second genuine stat-stuffer behind him, a center who already fills a box score like a ten-year vet.

Jamal Murray and Jaylen Brown are good real-NBA players whose profiles — lower blocks and steals, more volume-dependent scoring — just don't translate at a premium here. They're useful, not format-elite, and that gap between name recognition and actual Chabels production is the whole story of this roster's ceiling.

Under 26: Şengün himself is only 24, so the current best-case core has more runway than the record-book framing suggests. Behind him: Kon Knueppel (21, SF/SG, league #63), Brandon Miller (23, #69), Zach Edey (24, C, #79), and Ajay Mitchell (24, PG/SG, #111) — useful depth, nothing top-50 yet. The 2026 rookie class added three total stashes — Keaton Wagler (1.05, grade A-), Kingston Flemings (1.09, B+), and Hannes Steinbach (2.06, B) — all dev projects with no immediate role.

77-43 career, zero titles, perennial top-4. The eternal bridesmaid tag sticks until this roster actually closes one out.

CJ
PG Derrick White #20 · SG Derrick White #10 · SF Zion Williamson #14 · PF Kel'el Ware #20 · C Kel'el Ware #18

White and Zion are each quietly top-15 at their best spot — White's efficiency and steal rate play up at SG, and Zion's rebounding and two-point volume play up at SF when he's on the floor. Neither is a true format-elite centerpiece, and White's own primary spot (PG, #20) is just solid, not special.

The turnaround story is real on paper: 8 wins to 18 last season, and 17 incoming picks including two 2027 first-rounders. The next wave is coming — it's just not on this current lineup yet.

Under 26: This is where the roster actually sings. Kel'el Ware (22, C/PF, league #53) is already a top-20 center, and Keyonte George (22, #76), Stephon Castle (21, #78), Brandin Podziemski (23, #91), Cedric Coward (23, #107), and Christian Braun (25, #114) stack five more legitimate names under 26. The 2026 draft added Aday Mara (1.06, grade A-, the top center in the class) plus two late fliers in Isaiah Evans (4.02, B-) and Ryan Conwell (4.05, C). The picks get the headlines, but this core is the actual substance behind the rebuild.

The foundation is there. The house hasn't been built yet.

Schommer
PG Payton Pritchard #28 · SG Brandon Ingram #12 · SF Brandon Ingram #9 · PF Karl-Anthony Towns #5 · C Nikola Jokić #1

Jokić isn't just the best center in the league under this scoring — he's the single best asset, period. Assists, boards, efficiency, and 10-point triple-doubles whenever he feels like it. Karl-Anthony Towns stacks a second elite big behind him, and Brandon Ingram's two-way wing scoring rounds out a genuinely top-heavy roster.

The talent at the top was never the issue. Payton Pritchard, dead last among the league's rostered point guards, is. One all-format center can only do so much when the backcourt gives nothing back in assists or steals — the actual reason an 11, 16, 17-win climb hasn't translated to a real contender yet.

Under 26: Donovan Clingan (22, C, league #52) is a legitimate second big to pair with Jokić long after this era ends, and Ausar Thompson (23, SF/SG, #72) brings exactly the stocks-heavy defensive profile this format loves. Day'Ron Sharpe (24, #86), Jalen Green (24, #100), and Jalen Suggs (25, #117) add more names than most rosters can claim under 26. The 2026 class only added two late fliers, Henri Veesaar (3.10, grade C+) and Sergio De Larrea (4.04, C+) — neither projects as a difference-maker. Between the young bigs, Jokić, and KAT, this is still one of the few rosters with a real plan for the post-Jokić years whenever that day comes.

Fix the point guard spot and this team's ceiling is as high as anyone's. Jokić deserves better than a wasted prime.

Schu
PG Cooper Flagg #7 · SG Cooper Flagg #3 · SF Jalen Johnson #1 · PF Jalen Johnson #2 · C Pascal Siakam #9

Jalen Johnson is, improbably, the #1 power forward AND the #1 small forward league-wide at once — dual eligibility stacking real value. Cooper Flagg already ranks top-3 at SG as a rookie. Two format-elite young assets, on a team that just posted a 19-101 career record.

Three straight last-place finishes bought five first-round picks inside the top 20 of the 2026 draft. The losing was by design — and Johnson and Flagg both suggest the young core is further along than the record lets on.

Under 26: This is the youngest elite core in the league, full stop. Johnson himself is 24, Flagg is 19 and already a top-20 overall asset, Jalen Duren (22, C, league #29) gives a third genuine format-elite piece, and Chet Holmgren (24, C/PF, #32) and Dyson Daniels (23, SF/SG, #56) add two more. Nobody else in this league has five building blocks this young, this good, at once. And this roster didn't stop at the stars — Schu's 2026 class landed six rookies, more than any other team: AJ Dybantsa (1.02, grade A, already ranked #176 overall as a rookie), Dailyn Swain (2.03, B), Brayden Burries (2.05, B), Labaron Philon (2.07, B), Jayden Quaintance (2.10, B), and Joshua Jefferson (3.03, B-).

The tank worked. Now comes the part where the picks actually have to hit.

Noah
PG Donovan Mitchell #5 · SG Donovan Mitchell #2 · SF LeBron James #7 · PF LeBron James #15 · C Ivica Zubac #11

Mitchell is a real cornerstone, and LeBron still produces at SF even at this stage of his career. But LeBron's PF-eligible value thins out fast, and the roster's own flagged gap — power forward — is the single spot this scoring format punishes hardest to leave thin, since it's where most of the league's elite double-double/triple-double bigs live.

Second-best record in league history, zero championships. The PF hole might be exactly why the ceiling keeps closing in the games that actually matter.

Under 26: Josh Giddey (24, PG, league #21) quietly upgrades this section, but behind him it's thin: VJ Edgecombe (21, PG/SG, #71) is the only other piece with real upside, and Kyshawn George (22, #150), Tari Eason (25, #178), and Scoot Henderson (22, #186) are depth at best. The 2026 class added three rookies — Yaxel Lendeborg (1.10, grade B, already ranked #182 as a rookie), Nate Ament (2.09, B-), and Karim Lopez (3.01, B-) — none projecting as a near-term difference-maker. A roster built on Mitchell, LeBron, and Zubac is living entirely in the present — which is fine while it's winning big, but there's no obvious bridge to the next era once it stops.

Elite roster, elite wins, elite heartbreak. Fill the PF gap before it costs another deep run.

Nolan
PG Tyrese Haliburton #14 · SG Tyrese Haliburton #5 · SF Franz Wagner #13 · PF Franz Wagner #21 · C Victor Wembanyama #2

Wembanyama's block rate alone makes him close to unguardable in this exact format, before the rebounds and efficiency even factor in — arguably the scariest long-term building block in the league. Haliburton adds a real engine at guard, and Wagner rounds out the wing with his own two-way stat-stuffing profile.

No flagged roster gaps, which matters after the wildest run in league history: 17-23, then a 27-13 championship, then 10-30 the very next season. If this roster is genuinely stable again, the ring already in hand changes the ceiling conversation entirely.

Under 26: The best news on this roster is that its best player IS the young core — Wembanyama is 22 and already the #2 center in the league. Franz Wagner and Deni Avdija are both 25 and still fit the window, and Cason Wallace (22, league #124) is a modest fourth piece. The 2026 class delivered the headliner: Darryn Peterson (1.03, grade A, the top-ranked rookie guard) already projects at #157 overall, with Cameron Carr (2.08, B-) as a depth add. This isn't really a two-timeline problem: build around Wemby and Peterson now, and the timeline mostly takes care of itself.

The boom was historic. The bust was equally historic. Wemby buys this franchise patience either way.

Logan
PG Anthony Edwards #4 · SG Anthony Edwards #1 · SF Scottie Barnes #3 · PF Scottie Barnes #6 · C Isaiah Hartenstein #22

Edwards is the #1 shooting guard in the entire league under this scoring, and Barnes gives a real second format-elite wing. The top of this roster isn't the issue.

The price paid to win it all is: no first or second-round picks in 2026, and a flagged flex gap showing the bench thinned out on the way to the title. Defending the chip with no fresh draft capital walking in the door is a genuinely hard ask.

Under 26: Edwards and Barnes are both 25, so the actual stars still clear this bar. Beyond them: Tyrese Maxey (25, #19), Jalen Williams (25, #36), and LaMelo Ball (25, #47) are all still technically in-window, and Matas Buzelis (22, PF/SF, #64) and Collin Murray-Boyles (21, #115) add real frontcourt youth. What's missing entirely is 2026 rookie blood — having traded away the first- and second-rounders to win it all, this is one of only two rosters in the league (alongside Kaleb's) with zero names on the incoming rookie class. The bill for the title comes due in the draft room, not on the court.

80-40 all-time, a 34-6 title run, the best record in league history. Repeat or reload — and reload isn't really an option this year.

Kaleb
PG Luka Dončić #1 · SG Dylan Harper #28 · SF Miles Bridges #23 · PF Evan Mobley #8 · C Evan Mobley #6

The best record in franchise history (85-35) and zero rings — and on paper, this roster finally has the format fit to change that. Dončić's assist volume and efficiency print points here, and Mobley's block rate makes him worth meaningfully more under Chabels scoring than in a standard league.

The crack: Dylan Harper and Miles Bridges both rank in the bottom third at their spots. If Dončić or Mobley miss time, there's no second format-elite piece behind them — just depth. Deep bench (Mark Williams, Saddiq Bey, Toumani Camara) softens the blow but doesn't replace a star.

Under 26: Dončić (27) just misses this cut, but Mobley (25) doesn't, and Paolo Banchero (23, PF, league #28) is the real prize — a near-elite young forward who adds a genuine third star once he's fully healthy. Dylan Harper (20) is already rostered but still unproven at #80, Mark Williams (24, #103) and Gui Santos (24, #120) add bench-level youth, and Joan Beringer (19, C, #167) is a longer-term flier. Like Logan, this roster landed zero names in the 2026 rookie class — the young talent here was all acquired, not drafted.

The formula says contend. The history says something goes wrong in October — sorry, in the playoffs.

Christian/Mitch
PG Shai Gilgeous-Alexander #2 · SG CJ McCollum #29 · SF Andrew Wiggins #26 · PF Anthony Davis #7 · C Anthony Davis #5

SGA is about as clean a Chabels-scoring fit as exists — elite efficiency plus real free-throw volume means the fgm/fga and ftm/fta splits work entirely in his favor. Davis stacks a second stocks-and-boards monster behind him.

The soft spot is the wings: CJ McCollum and Andrew Wiggins both rank at the bottom of their positions — low block/steal rates and middling efficiency are exactly the profile this format doesn't reward, no matter what their real-NBA box scores say.

Under 26: Thin, and it shows. Caleb Wilson (20, PF/SF, league #89) and Derik Queen (21, C/PF, #92) are the headline names, with Moussa Diabaté (24, C, #142) adding a little more. The one bright spot in the 2026 class is Wilson himself — drafted at 1.04 with a grade A, one of the best-graded rookies in the entire class — plus two late depth adds in Bennett Stirtz (2.01, C) and Zuby Ejiofor (3.09, C+). With SGA and AD both past their mid-20s, this roster's best-case path still runs through winning now, not developing into something bigger — which tracks with a build that already spent its picks for one title.

One title on a zero-trade build, then a 14-26 hangover last year. SGA and AD are still good enough to answer "retool or rebuild?" with retool.

Austin
PG Amen Thompson #11 · SG Amen Thompson #4 · SF Jayson Tatum #2 · PF Jayson Tatum #3 · C Domantas Sabonis #7

Tatum and Sabonis give two legitimate format-first options, and Amen Thompson adds a third — stocks for days across the board. No flagged roster gaps.

The real story isn't a weakness on paper, it's volatility off it: two perfectly mediocre .500 seasons, then a 9-31 freefall. Owning every one of his own future picks means whichever direction this goes next is entirely Austin's call — no outside hand forcing the issue.

Under 26: Amen Thompson himself is only 23 and already a top-25 overall asset — the rare case of a roster's best present piece also being its best long-term one. Onyeka Okongwu (25, #42), Walker Kessler (25, #45), and Ryan Rollins (24, #75) stack up real depth, and Bilal Coulibaly (22, #194) adds a flier. The headline, though, is the 2026 draft: Cameron Boozer went 1.01 overall with a grade A+, the single best-graded rookie in the entire class, and already projects at league #68 as a rookie. Ebuka Okorie (2.02, C) and Bruce Thornton (4.06, C) round out the class. Between Thompson and Boozer, this is quietly one of the best-positioned rosters for the next five years in the league.

The pieces for a real format-fit core are already here. Somebody just needs to commit to a direction.
